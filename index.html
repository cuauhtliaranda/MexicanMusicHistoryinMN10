The first musical works by a Mexican artist to be published in Minnesota were composed during the Band's time in the state. Director Juan Macías created "Minneapolitan Flowers" and cornet soloist Gabriel Garzón composed "The Fair Minneapolitan." Originally performed at the Expo, and then published as sheet music for piano, Minnesotans played these compositions in their homes well after the Band returned home.

Though Mexicans have lived in Minnesota since the 1860s, the first known permanent resident was a nineteen-year-old oboe player named Luis Garzón.

Cornet soloist Gabriel Garzón, brother of Luis Garzón, composed this grand waltz during his stay in Minneapolis before returning home to Mexico, while Luis stayed in Minnesota. Though Mexicans have lived in Minnesota since the 1860s, the first known permanent resident was a nineteen-year-old oboe player named Luis Garzón. After falling ill, he stayed behind to recuperate when the Band returned to Mexico and then decided to make the Twin Cities his new home. Garzón married Clara Wagner, (the daughter of German immigrants), started a family in Minneapolis, became a United States citizen in 1900, and opened a grocery store in the early 1920's. Garzón's tienda on the West Side of St. Paul became an integral cultural hub where members of the burgeoning Mexican community could shop, meet neighbors, find support, and speak Spanish.
